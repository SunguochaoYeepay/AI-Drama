# Visual Quality Rules

## Character Lock

- Define age, body type, face character, hair, clothing layers, and dominant garment colors once.
- Keep those phrases identical across prompts. Do not replace a garment with a synonym midway through a story.
- Generate a neutral lineup with clear spacing, then use accepted references with Qwen Image Edit for scene frames.
- Reject celebrity resemblance, beauty retouching, fashion-editorial posing, age drift, duplicated people, and inconsistent clothing.

## Scene Continuity

- Fix the apartment layout, light source, time of day, table position, and recurring props.
- State left/right placement and eyelines when three or more people share a frame.
- Keep hands visible only when an action requires them; inspect every hand that holds a phone, pencil, bowl, or account book.
- Use restrained expressions at the opening so later emotional escalation remains visible.

## H3 Motion

- Animate one dominant action and at most two small reactions in a five-second test.
- Prefer a single shot and a small, slow push-in for dialogue scenes.
- Preserve subject identity, clothing, room geometry, and prop positions from the first frame.
- Avoid hard cuts until one-shot motion quality is accepted.
- Keep generated dialogue and music disabled when the accepted audio track will be added later.

## Review Checklist

- Faces are distinct, age-appropriate, and stable.
- No person, limb, finger, phone, pencil, or bowl is duplicated or malformed.
- Character placement supports the intended conflict and remains readable at mobile-video size.
- Lighting and color remain natural rather than glossy or heavily graded.
- The H3 clip contains motion throughout, no frozen or black frames, and no unwanted text.
- The final shot duration and frame rate match the planned audio segment.
